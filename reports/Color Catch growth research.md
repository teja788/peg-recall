# Win stars and searches before spending money

Color Catch's problem is too few downloads, not the price. At 42 downloads a month, the gaming benchmark of about **$0.14 revenue per install** predicts roughly **$6 a month and one purchase every three months**, so 0 purchases is the normal result, not a pricing failure ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps/)). Downloads leak at the search card. Only 1.9% of impressions become page views, and about 1% become downloads, against a US average install rate of about **3.8%** ([AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/average-app-conversion-rate-per-category?format=md)). Pages that people do open convert well. So the cheapest gains come from four things: putting stars on the search card (0 US ratings today), getting new players to a first win quickly so the 1.2.1 rating prompt actually fires, fixing keyword fields, and claiming the accessibility features Apple now displays. The best channel outside the store is the v1.3 WhatsApp share, which is already planned. It works better when the link opens a board people can play right away. Paid ads, portals, Amazon and Arcade all lose money or are closed at this scale. One finding comes from the repo rather than the research notes, and the owner should decide it this week: **19 of 20 store locales have keyword fields targeting "brain training" and dementia searches** (for example *gedächtnistraining*, *脳トレ*, *treino cerebral*, *asah otak*). That conflicts with the owner's no-brain-claims rule.

## Top 5 actions (do these first)

1. **Get your first 5–10 real US ratings, then reply to every review (about 1 h).** Ask US friends and testers for honest ratings, with no reward, because Apple bans incentives. Ratings count per country, so the stars from India don't show in the US, and search cards with 0 ratings show no stars. On one conversion curve, a 4★ app converts at 0.83× a 5★ app ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/ratings-and-reviews/); [NP Digital](https://neilpatel.com/marketing-stats/app-downloads-vs-ratings/)). *Impact: high.*
2. **Make the first launch a guided Small game against Bunny, skipping Home (about 1 day).** Opted-in D1 retention is about 1–4% against a 27–45% casual benchmark, and the 1.2.1 rating prompt only fires after a second happy game, so most players never see it. Check the cohort size first, because the opt-in sample may be tiny ([Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/mobile-gaming-app-user-retention-strategies.md); [Solsten](https://solsten.io/blog/d1-d7-d30-retention-in-gaming)). *Impact: high.*
3. **Keyword pass for the next version (about 2 h).**
   - Remove the brain-training words from every locale: *brain, cerveau, gedächtnistraining, hersentraining, 脳トレ, treino cerebral, trening mózgu, asah otak, ฝึกสมอง, luyện, تقوية*. Decide separately whether to keep *dementia* and *alzheimer*.
   - In en-GB, drop the words that repeat en-US (toddler, preschool, concentration, offline, board). en-GB is the second indexed locale in India and France, and repeated words add no coverage.
   - Keywords can only change with a version submission ([MobileAction](https://www.mobileaction.co/blog/app-store-cross-localization/); [FTC on Lumosity](https://ftc.gov/news-events/press-releases/2016/01/lumosity-pay-2-million-settle-ftc-deceptive-advertising-charges)).

   *Impact: medium on downloads. Removes a rule risk.*
4. **Declare Accessibility Nutrition Labels in App Store Connect (1–2 h including device testing).** Turn on VoiceOver, Larger Text, Reduce Motion and Differentiate Without Color, then play a full game with each. Declare a label only if the game passes with it. These labels will become mandatory, and they support the featuring nomination's accessibility angle ([App Store Connect Help](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/overview-of-accessibility-nutrition-labels); [Apple featuring criteria](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/getting-featured/)). *Impact: medium.*
5. **Add a "More from the developer" row to the water-reminder and Vivekananda apps (about 1 h).** Link to the App Store with `ct=xpromo` so these installs can be counted. The Vivekananda audience is in India and skews toward families and older users, which matches Color Catch. This is the only free channel you already own. The research found no benchmark for cross-promotion, and ad-based install exchanges would break the no-ads promise. *Impact: low to medium, guaranteed.*

## Don't do

- **Apple Search Ads.** The US median cost per tap is $1.91 (Games about $4), so a download costs about $3.80. Each download is worth about **$0.05–0.13** at a 2–5% unlock rate ([SplitMetrics](https://splitmetrics.com/blog/apple-search-ads-cost)).
- **Amazon Appstore.** It closed on Android in August 2025 and no longer accepts new apps ([Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/apps-software/amazon-will-suspend-support-for-its-appstore-on-android)).
- **Poki or CrazyGames.** Poki requires its own ad SDK and allows no external requests, which breaks the no-ads promise and store links ([Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/publish-game-poki)).
- **Apple Arcade for Color Catch.** Arcade games must not have been released before, and developers report bonus payments 2–6 months late ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2019/05/13/apple-arcade-developer-pitch/); [mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/inside-apple-arcade-again-late-payments-stonewalled-studios-terrible-tech-support-and-vision-pro-woes/)).
- **Online rooms, Game Center or an analytics SDK.** Any of these adds an identifier, which ends "Data Not Collected" and brings in COPPA's mixed-audience rules and India's DPDP parental consent ([Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect); [India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/dpdp-rules-2025-india-data-protection-law-compliance-40769.html)). Section 4b of the backlog proposes online rooms.
- **TikTok as the main video channel**, **Product Hunt or Show HN** (tech audiences), a **subscription**, **paid-upfront pricing**, **ads**, **rating resets**, **incentivized reviews**, and **"For Kids" wording** anywhere in the metadata.

## Why downloads, not monetization, are the lever

The revenue math settles the priorities. Gaming apps convert about **1.0%** of downloads to paid at the median, and India/SEA about **0.7%**. Revenue per install in India/SEA is a fifth of North America's ($0.11 vs $0.55 at D60) ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps/)). One US or French buyer is worth several Indian downloads, but the volume is too small for any price test to show a difference. The comparables point the same way. The closest match, Memory Games with Animals, is ad-free and unlocks content, and it built about **84K ratings over 13 years** of steady search presence, while AppGoblin estimates most memory apps at under $10K a month ([AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/631738108)). Small-team breakouts came from repeatable mechanics, not luck. Wordle grew from 90 to about 300,000 players in two months after it added a share grid ([Edinburgh News](https://www.edinburghnews.scotsman.com/news/world/wordle-snapped-up-by-the-new-york-times-in-low-seven-figure-deal-3550054)). Ludo King grew in India with no paid marketing, through family-group word of mouth and local languages that brought in players aged 45 and over ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/interview/67033/how-a-small-indian-studio-got-10-million-daus/); [afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/mktg/ludo-king-grew-400-in-2020-vikash-jaiswal-gametion-technologies)). Color Catch already has dice, play against the computer, pass-and-play and Hindi. What it lacks is the share loop and stars on its search card.

Some things remain uncertain. No public benchmark exists for one-time unlock conversion in kids' or family games. Apple has not confirmed that screenshot captions are indexed. No study quantifies how much in-app events, search-results images or WhatsApp links lift installs. Treat the impact labels above as ordinal judgments, and measure each change with `downloads.mjs` and campaign tokens.

## Compliance tripwires, ranked

Because Color Catch collects no data, sits outside the Kids Category and has a parental gate, its risk is low. Three things could raise it. The first is **wording**. The FTC's $2M Lumosity settlement over cognitive claims ([FTC](https://ftc.gov/news-events/press-releases/2016/01/lumosity-pay-2-million-settle-ftc-deceptive-advertising-charges)) and Apple's ban on unverifiable claims ([2.3.7](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)) are why the brain-training keywords should go. Hidden keywords are not visible claims, but they buy exactly the positioning the owner's rule forbids. Dementia and Alzheimer's terms are a closer call: caregivers search for them, and ranking there invites therapeutic expectations. The second is **data**: the daily board, the share and any future multiplayer must stay on device. The third is **nudging children**: under the UK children's code, a large "yes" button next to a small "no thanks" counts as a nudge ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/media/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services-2-1.pdf)), and that applies to the new upsell cards.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "how do we monetize better?" to "how do we get each search impression to show stars, a clear board and a reason to tap?" Most of the high-value fixes take under two hours and cost nothing. Several cut against the existing backlog: Common Sense Media has no submission route, Product Hunt reaches the wrong audience, online rooms would cost the app its strongest trust signal, and Google Play belongs right after v1.3 rather than "later". The next 10 days decide whether Color Catch gets a seasonal boost in India, because the Diwali event and nomination have to go in by about October 24.
